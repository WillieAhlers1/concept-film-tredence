Woodgrove small-business lending concept film narration (sample)

Host: This is a concept from Tredence for small-business lending. We'll follow one credit analyst, Adele Vance, at Woodgrove Bank. The bank, the people and the numbers are fictional.
Host: Adele's queue runs in arrival order, but the customer deadlines live in notes and emails.
Host: At two in the morning, while Adele sleeps, an agent plays her Tuesday forward.
Host: Worked in arrival order, two or three of her five deadline files are decided in time.
Host: So it tries fifteen hundred different orders, across three million simulated days, and keeps the one that decides all five in time.
Host: Tuesday morning. Adele gets in just before 7:30, with nine applications waiting.
Host: This time, she asks Copilot what to work on first.
Host: Copilot answers from the overnight plan. Northwind Traders' equipment loan comes first, due by 11:30.
Host: It drafts three document requests to the relationship managers.
Host: Nothing goes out until Adele approves, and every credit decision stays with her.
Host: Every morning starts from a plan built overnight, and every deadline file is decided in time.
Host: On illustrative numbers, expected lending per analyst-day goes from 1.17 million dollars to 1.37 million.
Host: The ask: let's run a 60-day shadow pilot with one lending team, measured against today's arrival order.
